\section{Specialization and the return to scissors congruence}
\label{cp:sec-specialization}

We now prove Theorem~\ref{cp:thm-main} from the universal vanishing theorem
and Inputs \textup{(E5)--(E8)}.  All arrows in the argument are named before
the diagram chase begins.

\subsection{The field-level comparison maps}

Let \(S_4(\C)_\Q\) be Goncharov's rational vector space of oriented
quadric simplices: generators are smooth oriented quadrics in
\(\mathbf P^3\) with four ordered hyperplane faces, modulo projective,
alternating, degeneracy, and additivity relations.  Its weight-two
differential is the reduced coproduct
\[
 \overline\Delta_\C:
 S_4(\C)_\Q\longrightarrow
 S_2(\C)_\Q\otimes_\Q S_2(\C)_\Q,
\]
where \(S_2(\C)_\Q\cong\C^*\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\Q\).  Since the normalized
cobar complex has no preceding term in this degree,
\begin{equation}
 H^1(S^\bullet(2))_\Q=\ker\overline\Delta_\C.
 \label{cp:eq-primitive-quadric-group}
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}[External: Goncharov's weight-two comparison]
\label{cp:thm-goncharov-weight-two}
There is an injective linear map
\begin{equation}
 c_G:\ker\overline\Delta_\C
 \longrightarrow \gr_2^\gamma K_3(\C)\otimes\Q.
 \label{cp:eq-goncharov-k3-map}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

This is the case \(n=2,i=1\) of Goncharov's Theorem~4.13 and equations
\textup{(4.6)--(4.7)} \cite{Goncharov1999}.  It is Input
\textup{(E6)}; the manuscript does not formalize algebraic \(K\)-theory or
reprove injectivity.

Define the reduced spherical scissors group by
\[
 \Pred(S^3)=\operatorname{coker}
 \bigl(\Sigma:\Pfull(S^2)\longrightarrow\Pfull(S^3)\bigr),
\]
where \(\Sigma\) is spherical suspension.  Geometrically, if a polygon lies
in an equatorial \(S^2\), its suspension is the double cone obtained by
joining it to the two antipodal poles.  Cutting makes this construction an
additive map on scissors groups.

\begin{theorem}[External: spherical presentation and quadric map]
\label{cp:lem-spherical-presentations}
The reduced geometric group \(\Pred(S^3)\) is canonically the spherical
configuration group used by Goncharov.  After rationalization,
complexification gives an injective map
\begin{equation}
 \psi_S:\Pred(S^3)\otimes\Q
 \lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow S_{4,\Q}^{\mathrm{sph}},
 \label{cp:eq-spherical-quadric-map}
\end{equation}
where \(S_{4,\Q}^{\mathrm{sph}}\) is the conjugation-invariant spherical
part of \(S_4(\C)_\Q\) with its one-dimensional parity line.  The parity
line records how reversing the chosen ruling changes an oriented quadric
generator; tensoring with that line is what makes the map descend from the
unoriented geometric scissors group fixed in Section~1.  For a
tetrahedron with angle Gram matrix \(G\), its image is represented by
\(Q_{G^{-1}}\) with its ordered coordinate hyperplanes.
\end{theorem}

The presentation comparison, including its value on this tetrahedral
generator and hence on the Regge difference, is Input~\textup{(E7)}, cited from Dupont's
Theorem~7.14 \cite{Dupont2001}; the map and its injectivity are Input
\textup{(E6)}, cited from Goncharov's Theorem~4.14
\cite{Goncharov1999}.  We suppress the nonzero one-dimensional parity
factor when testing whether a class vanishes.

Projective duality gives an involutive linear automorphism
\[
 D:S_4(\C)_\Q\xrightarrow{\sim}S_4(\C)_\Q
\]
which takes the \(Q_{G^{-1}}\)-class to the coordinate \(Q_G\)-class.
Input~\textup{(E6)} includes both this map and its compatibility with the
two displayed Gram-matrix generators.  Consequently
\(D(x)=0\) if and only if \(x=0\).

\subsection{The family-to-fibre bridge}

The last comparison is not a formal consequence of merely having a
pullback functor.

\begin{proposition}[External: fibre endpoint comparison]
\label{cp:lem-fibre-comparison}
Let \(s:\Spec\C\to U\) be a complex point and let
\(\delta_s^\vee\in S_4(\C)_\Q\) be the projectively dual
quadric-scissors defect.  If it is primitive, then the specialization of
the endpoint of the relative coefficient defect is exactly
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{endpoint}_s(\delta_U^\vee)
 =c_G(\delta_s^\vee).
 \label{cp:eq-fibre-endpoint}
\end{equation}
The equation uses the same face-complex signs, ruling frame, ordered-face
frame, projective duality, and Tate twist on both sides.
\end{proposition}

The specialization and endpoint arrows in this equation are linear maps.
This proposition is Input~\textup{(E5)}.  Goncharov's face-motive
construction over a field suggests it \cite{Goncharov1999}, and
Definition~\ref{cp:def-face-motive} fixes the candidate relative object.
The exact relative base-change and normalization comparison is not proved
in this manuscript and is not attributed to a field-level theorem.

\subsection{The machine-checkable diagram chase}

Let \(T,T^R\) be the pair in Theorem~\ref{cp:thm-main}.  By
Proposition~\ref{cp:prop-physical-point-on-chart}, their angle phases and
positive determinant root define a point
\[
 s_T:\Spec\C\longrightarrow U.
\]
Put
\[
 z=[T]-[T^R]\in\Pfull(S^3),\qquad
 \bar z\in\Pred(S^3),
\]
and let
\[
 \delta_{s_T}=\psi_S(\bar z\otimes1),\qquad
 \delta_{s_T}^\vee=D(\delta_{s_T}).
\]

\begin{proposition}[Reduced scissors vanishing]
\label{cp:prop-reduced-scissors-zero}
Under Inputs \textup{(E1)--(E8)},
\[
 \bar z=0\qquad\text{in }\Pred(S^3).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Lemma~\ref{cp:lem-coproduct-zero} gives
\(\overline\Delta_\C(\delta_{s_T}^\vee)=0\), so
\(\delta_{s_T}^\vee\) lies in the domain of \(c_G\).
Theorem~\ref{cp:thm-relative-defect-zero} gives
\(\delta_U^\vee=0\).  The endpoint map is linear, hence its value on this
zero class is zero.  Proposition~\ref{cp:lem-fibre-comparison} therefore
gives
\[
 c_G(\delta_{s_T}^\vee)=0.
\]
Injectivity in Theorem~\ref{cp:thm-goncharov-weight-two} yields
\(\delta_{s_T}^\vee=0\).  Since \(D\) is an involution,
\(\delta_{s_T}=0\).  Injectivity of \(\psi_S\) gives
\[
 \bar z\otimes1=0\quad\text{in }\Pred(S^3)\otimes\Q.
\]
By Input~\textup{(E8)}, \(\Pfull(S^2)\) and \(\Pfull(S^3)\) are
\(\Q\)-vector spaces.  The image of suspension and its cokernel
\(\Pred(S^3)\) are therefore \(\Q\)-vector spaces as well.  The canonical
map \(\Pred(S^3)\to\Pred(S^3)\otimes\Q\) is injective, so
\(\bar z=0\).
\end{proof}

This proof is only a composition of functions, preservation of zero, and
injectivity.  Lean proves it parametrically from the fields of the external
package; it does not use the names of the geometric theories to bypass any
arrow.

\subsection{Removing suspension}

Because \(\bar z=0\) in the cokernel of suspension, there is
\(Q\in\Pfull(S^2)\) such that
\begin{equation}
 z=\Sigma Q.
 \label{cp:eq-regge-suspension}
\end{equation}
Input~\textup{(E8)} includes the suspension-volume identity.  Its
normalization can be seen on a polygon directly: suspension from the two
poles has warped-product volume element
\(\cos^2(t)\,dt\,dA\), where
\(-\pi/2\leq t\leq\pi/2\).  Hence
\begin{equation}
 \Vol(\Sigma Q)
 =\left(\int_{-\pi/2}^{\pi/2}\cos^2t\,dt\right)\Area(Q)
 =\frac\pi2\Area(Q).
 \label{cp:eq-suspension-volume}
\end{equation}
Both volume and area are additive under cutting, so this formula extends
from polygons to every class in \(\Pfull(S^2)\).  The Lean proof does not
formalize this integration; it consumes the resulting additive identity as
the named field \texttt{suspension\_volume} of Input~\textup{(E8)}.

By Input~\textup{(E1)}, Regge mates have equal volume.  Applying volume to
Equation~\eqref{cp:eq-regge-suspension} and using
\eqref{cp:eq-suspension-volume} gives \(\Area(Q)=0\).  Input
\textup{(E8)} says that area is injective on \(\Pfull(S^2)\); hence
\(Q=0\), so \(z=0\) in \(\Pfull(S^3)\).  Its cancellation clause turns
this equality into a finite equidecomposition.  This proves
Theorem~\ref{cp:thm-main}.

\subsection{Logical inventory}

The last three sections now contain no hidden motivic inference.  The
internal implications are:
\[
\begin{gathered}
 H^tH=4I
 \Longrightarrow
 \text{four moving tensors agree},\\
 \overline\Delta_U(\delta_U^\vee)=0
 \ \&\
 \ker\overline\Delta_U\cong0
 \Longrightarrow
 \delta_U^\vee=0,\\
 \delta_U^\vee=0
 \Longrightarrow c_G(\delta_{s_T}^\vee)=0
 \Longrightarrow \bar z=0
 \Longrightarrow z=0.
\end{gathered}
\]
Every implication not reducible to this finite algebra or diagram chase is
an explicitly numbered external input in
Subsection~\ref{cp:subsec-external-contract}.
