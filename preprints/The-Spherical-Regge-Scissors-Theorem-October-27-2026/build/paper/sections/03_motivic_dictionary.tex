\section{The motivic dictionary and the exact interface}
\label{cp:sec-motivic-dictionary}

This section explains the intended geometry behind Inputs
\textup{(E2)--(E5)} and then restates those inputs as ordinary linear
algebra.  It is intentionally not presented as a construction of a relative
category of motives.  A reader interested only in the logical proof may
start at Subsection~\ref{cp:subsec-linear-contract}.

\subsection{From a spherical simplex to an oriented quadric simplex}
\label{cp:subsec-quadric-simplex}

Let \(U\) be one of the smooth rational opens of Section
\ref{cp:sec-phase-charts}.  Over it we have a rank-four trivial bundle and
two symmetric invertible matrices \(G,G^R\).  The equation
\[
 Q_G:\quad z^tGz=0
\]
defines a relative quadric in \(\mathbf P^3_U\); the four coordinate
hyperplanes \(M_i=\{z_i=0\}\) are its ordered faces.

The spherical tetrahedron first produces \(Q_{G^{-1}}\).  Projective
duality replaces it by \(Q_G\): if \(x=Gy\), then
\[
 x^tG^{-1}x=y^tGy,
 \qquad e_j^tG^{-1}x=y_j.
\]
Thus the polar faces become coordinate faces.  This elementary change of
coordinates explains why Section~\ref{cp:sec-relative-rigidity} calculates
with \(G\), although Goncharov's spherical generator is written using
\(G^{-1}\).  Input~\textup{(E6)} includes the assertion that projective
duality is an involution on the relevant quadric-scissors group.

A smooth quadric surface over an algebraically closed field has two
families of maximal isotropic lines, called its two rulings.  An orientation
is a choice between them.  For a matrix quadric, a square root
\(w^2=\det G\) distinguishes the choices; changing \(w\) interchanges them.
This is why the parameter space in Section~\ref{cp:sec-phase-charts} is the
determinant double cover.

\subsection{What a motive is}
\label{cp:subsec-what-is-a-motive}

Ordinary cohomology sends a space directly to groups or vector spaces.  A
motive is intended to be a universal algebraic object from which several
cohomology theories---Betti, de Rham, Hodge, or \'{e}tale, for
example---are obtained by realization functors.  One keeps the object
itself because the proof needs operations which a list of Betti numbers
forgets: pullback in a family, tensor products, duality, Tate twists,
localization triangles, filtrations, and extension classes.

The customary ambient notation is
\[
 \DM(U)_\Q.
\]
Morally, this is a rational, symmetric monoidal, stable category generated
by smooth \(U\)-schemes and localization.  The unit is \(\Q_U(0)\); the Tate
object is \(\Q_U(1)\); shifts are denoted by \([m]\).  In such a category
motivic cohomology is the Hom group
\begin{equation}
 H^m(U,\Q(n))=
 \operatorname{Hom}_{\DM(U)_\Q}
 \bigl(\Q_U(0),\Q_U(n)[m]\bigr).
 \label{cp:eq-motivic-cohomology-definition}
\end{equation}
This formula explains the notation used later.  It does not, by itself,
construct the category or prove any comparison theorem.

Why not replace every motive by a complex of rational vector spaces?  That
replacement destroys the obstruction on which the proof depends.  The
category of rational vector spaces is semisimple, so
\[
 \operatorname{Ext}^1_{\operatorname{Vect}_\Q}(\Q,\Q)=0,
\]
and every bounded complex is quasi-isomorphic to the direct sum of its
cohomology groups.  A nontrivial motivic extension
\[
 0\longrightarrow\Q(2)\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow
 \Q(0)\longrightarrow0
\]
would therefore become split after forgetting to plain vector spaces.
The lost extension is precisely the dilogarithmic or \(K_3\) information
that distinguishes a primitive weight-two class.  Motives are not ornate
terminology for ordinary homology; they retain the extension data which
ordinary rational homology cannot see.

The conventions relevant here are
\[
 H^1(W,\Q(1))\cong\mathcal O(W)^*\otimes_{\mathbf Z}\Q,
 \qquad
 H^1(F,\Q(2))\cong\gr_2^\gamma K_3(F)\otimes\Q
\]
for the schemes and fields to which the external comparison theorems apply.
The first formula turns cross-ratios into weight-one classes.  The second
explains why the endpoint obstruction belongs to \(K_3\).

Here \(K_3(F)\) is Quillen's third algebraic \(K\)-group: it is the third
homotopy group of the algebraic \(K\)-theory space built from invertible
matrices over \(F\).  Exterior-power operations give its gamma filtration;
\(\gr_2^\gamma K_3(F)\) is the second associated quotient of that
filtration.  No presentation or computation of the full group is needed in
this proof.  The notation \(\Ext^1(X,Y)\) means equivalence classes of short
exact sequences
\[
 0\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow X\longrightarrow0.
\]
Thus the proposed class in \(H^1(U,\Q(2))\) records a possibly nonsplit
extension between the two endpoint Tate objects.

\subsection{Tate and sign-Artin objects}

The symbol \(\Q_U(n)\) denotes the \(n\)-fold Tate twist.  The indices
\(0,1,2\) below are Tate degrees; they are not homological degrees.  A
Tate object is the basic one-dimensional motivic object corresponding in
degree one to the multiplicative group; tensoring by it shifts Tate degree.
An Artin object is the motive of a finite \'{e}tale scheme and, over a field,
behaves like a rational representation of its finite Galois group.  A
quadratic finite \'{e}tale cover \(\pi:U_p\to U\), with deck involution
\(\sigma\), is expected to give
\begin{equation}
 \pi_*\Q_{U_p}\cong\Q_U\oplus\chi,
 \qquad
 \chi=\operatorname{im}\frac{1-\sigma}{2}.
 \label{cp:eq-sign-motive}
\end{equation}
The summand \(\chi\) is the sign-Artin object.  If the cover splits, this
summand is another copy of \(\Q_U\).  Abstractly the projector exists
because \(2\) is invertible over \(\Q\).  The assertion that this
decomposition, pullback, transfer, and the relevant framed coefficient
spaces are all compatible is the Artin-trace clause of Input
\textup{(E4)}.

\subsection{The intended four-term face object}
\label{cp:subsec-face-complex}

For \(I\subset\{0,1,2,3\}\), put
\[
 M_I=\bigcap_{i\in I}M_i,\qquad Q_I=Q\cap M_I.
\]
Ordering the hyperplanes gives alternating incidence signs.  The geometric
expression behind the relative coefficient is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 \mathcal F_U(Q,M):\quad
 \bigoplus_{|I|=3}M_U(M_I\setminus Q_I)
 &\longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{|I|=2}M_U(M_I\setminus Q_I)\\
 &\longrightarrow
 \bigoplus_{|I|=1}M_U(M_I\setminus Q_I)
 \longrightarrow M_U(\mathbf P^3_U\setminus Q).
\end{split}
 \label{cp:eq-geometric-face-complex}
\end{equation}
Here \(M_U(X)\) denotes the proposed geometric motive of a smooth
\(U\)-scheme \(X\).  Totalization is the stable-category analogue of forming
one object from a bounded chain complex; it may be described by successive
mapping cones.  The internal Hom into \(\Q_U\) is its cohomological dual,
and the final \((2)\) is the normalization which places the endpoint Tate
degrees at \(0\) and \(2\).
If a relative motivic category with functorial totalizations and duals has
been fixed, the intended normalized object is
\begin{definition}[Intended normalized relative quadric motive]
\label{cp:def-face-motive}
\begin{equation}
 \mathfrak m_U(Q,M)=
 \underline{\operatorname{Hom}}
 \bigl(\operatorname{Tot}\mathcal F_U(Q,M),
       \Q_U\bigr)(2).
 \label{cp:eq-normalized-relative-motive}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Definition~\ref{cp:def-face-motive} fixes the signs, dual, and Tate twist
which Inputs \textup{(E2)--(E5)} must use.  It is a specification, not a
claim that the indicated totalization has been constructed in Lean or that
all of its relative comparison properties follow formally from a citation.
Its field-level face-complex convention is the one used by Goncharov
\cite{Goncharov1999}; the twist agrees with Brown's normalization
\cite[Definition~5.8]{Brown2013}.  The Aomoto-pair construction of
Beilinson--Goncharov--Schechtman--Varchenko is a historical precursor
\cite{BGSV1990}.  None of these citations is being used as an unstated proof
of the relative base-change assertions in Input~\textup{(E2)}.

The expected localization calculation has three graded pieces:
\begin{equation}
 \gr^0\mathfrak m_U\cong\Q_U(0),\qquad
 \gr^1\mathfrak m_U\cong
   \bigoplus_{|I|=2}\widetilde M(Q_I)(1),\qquad
 \gr^2\mathfrak m_U\cong\Q_U(2).
 \label{cp:eq-dictionary-three-graded-pieces}
\end{equation}
Here \(\widetilde M(Q_I)\) is the reduced degree-two Artin object of the
binary section.  In the Regge family five such objects are split and the
sixth is either split or \(\chi\).  The orientation supplies a frame on one
endpoint and the ordered faces supply a frame on the other.  The existence
of a strict heart containing these objects, the filtration
\eqref{cp:eq-dictionary-three-graded-pieces}, and its compatibility with
the frames are exactly the non-formal part of Input~\textup{(E3)}.

A \(t\)-structure on a stable category selects an abelian \emph{heart} in which
appropriate triangles become short exact sequences.  A three-weight heart
here would be the extension closure of the three kinds of pure object in
Equation~\eqref{cp:eq-dictionary-three-graded-pieces}.  A framing chooses
the generator of the top \(\Q(0)\)-piece and the covector on the bottom
\(\Q(2)\)-piece.  The reduced coproduct of the resulting matrix coefficient
records all factorizations from the top frame to the bottom frame through
the middle degree.  Consequently a zero reduced coproduct is called
\emph{primitive}: no middle factorization remains, so the only possible
datum is the direct endpoint extension.  Input~\textup{(E3)} asserts that
this description is valid for the proposed relative objects.

\subsection{The linear contract used by the proof}
\label{cp:subsec-linear-contract}

Nothing after this point requires manipulating a category of motives.  The
conditional proof uses the following data.

\begin{definition}[Weight-two coefficient contract]
\label{cp:def-coefficient-contract}
For a chart \(U\), a weight-two coefficient contract consists of rational
vector spaces
\[
 A_{02}(U),\qquad A_{11}(U),\qquad H_U,
\]
a linear map
\[
 \overline\Delta_U:A_{02}(U)\longrightarrow A_{11}(U),
\]
an isomorphism
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{prim}_U:
 \ker\overline\Delta_U\xrightarrow{\sim}H_U,
 \label{cp:eq-primitive-is-ext}
\end{equation}
and a proof that \(H_U=0\).  It also contains the two framed coefficients
\[
 c_U(G),\qquad c_U(G^R),
\]
the six-channel coproduct formula of Input~\textup{(E4)}, a linear
specialization map on the middle coproduct which commutes with the
field-level quadric coproduct, and the linear coefficient-specialization and
endpoint maps of Input~\textup{(E5)}.  The field-level map \(c_G\) is typed
only on the kernel of that field-level coproduct; primitivity is therefore a
proof obligation, not a suggestive name attached to an arbitrary subgroup.
\end{definition}

The intended interpretation is
\[
 H_U=
 \Ext^1\bigl(\Q_U(0),\Q_U(2)\bigr)
 =H^1(U,\Q(2)).
\]
Calling an element primitive means only that its reduced coproduct is zero.
The following deduction is elementary and is the part formalized in Lean.

\begin{lemma}[Primitive vanishing]
\label{cp:lem-three-level-framed}
In a weight-two coefficient contract, if
\(\overline\Delta_U(x)=0\), then \(x=0\).
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The equation lets us regard \(x\) as an element
\(\widetilde x\in\ker\overline\Delta_U\).  Its image under
\(\operatorname{prim}_U\) lies in the zero vector space \(H_U\), hence is
zero.  Since \(\operatorname{prim}_U\) is injective, \(\widetilde x=0\), and
therefore \(x=0\).
\end{proof}

This lemma is intentionally weaker than constructing a motivic heart.  The
deep assertion is that the geometric coefficient contract exists and that
\(\operatorname{prim}_U\) is the motivic primitive--extension
identification.  That assertion is Input~\textup{(E3)}.

\subsection{Why the proposed obstruction vanishes}

The suggested mathematical route to the last field of the contract is
\begin{equation}
\begin{split}
 H_U
 &=H^1(U,\Q(2))\\
 &\cong H^1(\Q(t_1,\ldots,t_6),\Q(2))\\
 &\cong H^1(\Q,\Q(2))\\
 &\cong\gr_2^\gamma K_3(\Q)\otimes\Q=0.
\end{split}
\label{cp:eq-weight-two-rigidity-route}
\end{equation}
The middle comparisons are the intended application of Garkusha's
birational and pure-transcendental results \cite{Garkusha2023}; the final
vanishing uses Borel's rank theorem \cite{Borel1977}.  For the conditional
proof, Equation~\eqref{cp:eq-weight-two-rigidity-route} is not rederived
inside an unspecified category \(\DM(U)_\Q\).  Its exact applicability to
the chosen open and to the heart-to-derived comparison is bundled into
Input~\textup{(E3)}.

\subsection{Specialization and the boundary of the interface}

At a complex point \(s:\Spec\C\to U\), the intended motivic construction
would provide a pullback
\[
 s^*\mathfrak m_U(Q,M)\cong\mathfrak m_\C(Q_s,M_s)
\]
preserving totalization, internal duality, twist, both frames, the
three-step filtration, and the endpoint map.  Goncharov's construction over
\(\C\) would then identify that endpoint with \(c_G\) of the corresponding
primitive quadric class.

This is exactly Input~\textup{(E5)}.  The manuscript does not infer it from
an unspecified appeal to functoriality.  In particular, the following five
assertions remain on the external side of the proof:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.4em]
\item construction of the required relative \(\DM(U)_\Q\) objects and
      their functorial totalizations;
\item the relative localization calculation and strict three-level
      filtration;
\item primitive coefficients \(=\Ext^1=H^1(U,\Q(2))\);
\item twisted-trace compatibility for the sign-Artin channel; and
\item coproduct-compatible specialization, followed by equality of the
      specialized endpoint, with all signs and twists, to
      Goncharov's field-level map \(c_G\).
\end{enumerate}

The rest of the manuscript never uses a stronger motivic statement than
these five clauses.
